# Trading Glossary

This glossary explains the main forex, technical-analysis, trade-management, macroeconomic, AI, and risk terms used by the Forex AI Intelligence Engine.

## Market Basics

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Forex | Foreign exchange market where currency pairs are traded. |
| Currency Pair | Two currencies quoted together, such as EUR/USD. |
| Base Currency | First currency in a pair; the unit being priced. |
| Quote Currency | Second currency in a pair; it expresses the price. |
| Bid | Price at which the market buys the base currency from you. |
| Ask | Price at which the market sells the base currency to you. |
| Spread | Difference between ask and bid. |
| Pip | Standard small FX price increment used to measure movement. |
| Lot | Standardized trading quantity; broker contract sizes can differ. |
| Leverage | Broker-provided exposure relative to account equity. |
| Margin | Collateral required to maintain leveraged exposure. |

## Technical Analysis

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ATR | Average True Range, a volatility measure used for dynamic SL distance. |
| Support | Price area where buying interest may slow or reject declines. |
| Resistance | Price area where selling interest may slow or reject advances. |
| Trend | Persistent directional price movement. |
| Range | Market condition where price oscillates within a bounded area. |
| Breakout | Movement beyond a meaningful support or resistance area. |
| Volatility | Degree and speed of price variation over time. |
| Candlestick | Price bar containing open, high, low, and close. |
| Timeframe | Chart interval used to aggregate market prices. |

## Trade Management

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Entry | Planned price at which a trade is opened. |
| Stop Loss (SL) | Predefined exit intended to limit trade loss. |
| Take Profit (TP) | Predefined exit intended to capture a planned gain. |
| Risk/Reward (R:R) | Potential reward divided by amount risked. |
| Risk Distance | Absolute price distance between entry and SL. |
| Position Size | Quantity traded for a defined risk budget. |
| Break-even Stop | Stop moved to entry, optionally with an offset. |
| Trailing Stop | Stop that follows favorable movement while limiting reversal risk. |
| R-Multiple | Trade result expressed as a multiple of initial risk. |
| Slippage | Difference between intended and actual execution price. |

## Fundamental & Macro

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fundamental Analysis | Assessment using economic, financial, and macro factors. |
| Interest Rate | Central-bank policy rate affecting currency valuation and funding. |
| CPI | Consumer Price Index, a measure of consumer price inflation. |
| GDP | Gross Domestic Product, a broad measure of economic output. |
| Employment Data | Labor-market indicators affecting policy expectations. |
| Central Bank | Institution responsible for monetary-policy functions. |
| Economic Calendar | Schedule of economic releases and central-bank events. |

## AI, Backtesting & Risk

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ML | Machine learning models that learn patterns from historical data. |
| Probability | Model-estimated likelihood for a defined event or direction. |
| Confidence | Measure of separation or certainty in a model output. |
| Signal | Structured evidence for a possible direction or neutral state. |
| Market Regime | Current state such as trending, ranging, high-volatility, or low-volatility. |
| Risk Filter | Rule or score that can reduce or block a trade. |
| Walk-forward Validation | Time-ordered validation designed to avoid future-data leakage. |
| Look-ahead Bias | Use of information that would not have been known at trade time. |
| Backtest | Historical simulation under defined assumptions. |
| Drawdown | Decline from an equity peak to a later trough. |
| Expectancy | Average expected profit or loss per trade over repeated observations. |
| Profit Factor | Gross winning profit divided by gross losing loss. |

> **Important:** Definitions are educational. Broker specifications, instrument conventions, execution conditions, and local regulations can differ.


## Core 7 Major Currency Pairs

These seven pairs are included as the engine's **core educational major-pair universe**:

| Pair | Description |
|---|---|
| **EUR/USD** | Euro vs US dollar. One of the core global major pairs. |
| **USD/JPY** | US dollar vs Japanese yen. Closely associated with Asian-session trading. |
| **GBP/USD** | British pound vs US dollar. Often known for relatively large price movements. |
| **AUD/USD** | Australian dollar vs US dollar. Sensitive to Australian/global commodity conditions. |
| **USD/CHF** | US dollar vs Swiss franc. CHF is widely used as a safe-haven currency. |
| **USD/CAD** | US dollar vs Canadian dollar. CAD is sensitive to energy and oil conditions. |
| **NZD/USD** | New Zealand dollar vs US dollar. Sensitive to New Zealand and global commodity conditions. |

### Important market-data note

“Major pairs” is a conventional educational classification, not a fixed official list. The BIS 2025 survey reports that the US dollar was on one side of 89.2% of global FX trades in April 2025, and the ten most-traded currency pairs all involved USD. ## Precious Metals

Precious metals should be represented separately from the seven currency pairs:

| Symbol | Instrument | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **XAU/USD** | Gold | Gold quoted in US dollars. |
| **XAG/USD** | Silver | Silver quoted in US dollars. |

CME's FX product guide lists XAU/USD and XAG/USD as spot precious-metal instruments. Therefore, **“AGU” should be corrected to “XAG”** when referring to silver. citeturn0search11

For the Forex AI engine, this distinction is useful because currency pairs and metals can have different tick sizes, contract specifications, volatility characteristics, margin rules, and broker symbol conventions.
